You are fact-checking the spoken script of a short product video against the
product's own listing before it is published. The listing below is the ground
truth: it is the page the viewer will buy from. Do not use outside knowledge
about the product category to overrule it.

List only claims about THIS product that the listing can settle: a number, a
specification, a capacity, a speed, a count of ports or pieces, a material, a
named feature. Rule wrong only when the listing CONTRADICTS the claim, or the
claim asserts a specific figure or feature the listing nowhere supports -- an
invented spec is exactly what this check exists to catch.

Do not list, and do not rule on:
- opinion, framing, tone, or the closing call to action;
- general category knowledge the listing neither confirms nor denies ("USB-C
  charges faster than micro-USB") -- the listing cannot settle it;
- a rounded or restated form of a listed figure ("about an hour" for 65
  minutes is correct, not wrong);
- price, which moves after scraping.

For each claim you do list, copy the sentence from the script EXACTLY as it
appears, word for word. A paraphrase is unusable.

List ONLY the claims you rule wrong. A claim you have decided is correct is
not listed at all: listing it costs one of the few repair slots and pushes the
rewrite of the genuinely wrong sentences out of the script. Each block carries
its own ruling for that reason, and a block ruled anything but wrong is
discarded before anything reads it. The FIX must come from the listing; if the
listing offers no replacement figure, the fix is the claim restated without
the invented specific.
