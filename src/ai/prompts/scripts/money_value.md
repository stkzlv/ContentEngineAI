# Script Template: Money Value

Write a voiceover script for a short product promo video focused entirely on the price-to-value ratio. The angle is simple: this product punches above its price tag.

Open by anchoring the price. Put it out there early so the rest of the script makes the viewer rethink what that number gets them. Something like "For {PRICE}, you'd expect maybe one or two decent features. Not this." or "I've paid twice this for half the product." Don't be shy about naming the actual price.

Then stack the value. Pull 2-3 specific features or specs from the product description below and frame each one in terms of what it would cost elsewhere or what you'd normally have to give up at this price point. "Usually at this range you're choosing between X and Y. This one just does both." Make each point feel like the deal gets better and better.

Close by putting a bow on the value argument. One line that sums up why the price-to-product ratio is worth paying attention to. Then a quick CTA: follow, like, link in bio, or share.

## Rules

- Write the actual spoken words only. No stage directions, no scene descriptions, no camera notes.
- Target 30-40 seconds at a normal speaking pace (roughly 75-100 words).
- No emojis, no hashtags, no greetings like "hey guys", no markdown formatting in the output.
- No marketing buzzwords: don't say "game changer", "next-level", "ultimate", "must-have", "revolutionary", or similar.
- Clean plain text, suitable for direct text-to-speech input.
- Refer to the product by a short natural name, not the full official title.
- You must reference the price at least once. Use {PRICE} naturally in the script, not as a footnote.
- Don't trash competing products by name. Compare against general expectations at the price range, not specific brands.
- Avoid sounding like an infomercial. "But wait, there's more" energy is exactly what you don't want.
- The tone is someone who found a genuinely good deal and wants to pass it along. Helpful, not salesy.
- Contractions, varied sentence length, fragments are all fine. Keep it conversational.
