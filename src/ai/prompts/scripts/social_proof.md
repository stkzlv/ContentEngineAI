# Script Template: Social Proof

Write a voiceover script for a short product promo video built on the listing's rating and ratings count.

The listing shows a rating of {PRODUCT_RATING} from {REVIEW_COUNT} ratings. Open with that figure AND name the product category or audience cue in the same sentence (see Rules below for the full spec). Shapes: "[count] ratings and a [rating] average for a [category]." / "A [category] holding [rating] stars over [count] ratings." Quote the figures exactly as given here and nothing else about other people: no friends, feeds, trends or "everyone".

Then explain what could earn that rating. Pull 2-3 specific features from the product description below and frame them as the likely reasons. Tie each feature to why a buyer would care, not abstract praise.

End on what the rating means for the viewer deciding, then one CTA per the narrator profile.

## Rules

- The rating and ratings count above are the only proof. Quote them as given and cite no other popularity figure, person or trend.
- Don't make it sound like an infomercial. The tone is someone reading the ratings summary carefully, not a salesperson.
- Casual and measured: a high rating over few ratings is weaker than a slightly lower one over many.
- **Open with a natural conversational hook that carries the audio keyword** (product category, audience cue, pain point) in speech a person would actually say, never as a search-bar query. TikTok 2026 indexes ASR transcripts as a primary search signal, so the keyword must land in the first 5 seconds of TTS. Pick one of five proven shapes: contrarian ("Don't buy a [category] until you check [spec]"), POV, outcome-first, numbered teardown, comparison. Never speak a price. Word budget ≤3 seconds, ~5-12 words. Anti-pattern: "Best [X] for [Y]" reads as a Google query. Name the brand and a concrete spec by the second sentence at the latest. Read it out loud; if it sounds like a search bar, rewrite. Anti-setup: line 1 states a concrete fact, result, or observation about the product or its use, not a description of what is about to be shown. Avoid "Today I'll", "Let me tell you", "In this video", "I want to share" framings; they read as setup and burn the 1.5-second decision window.
- **Close with a two-option opinion question right before the CTA. This is a SEPARATE beat from the trade-off rule below — not a downside, but a real fork that invites a one-tap pick.** Shapes: "[option A] or [option B] - which matters more for you?" / "Team [option A] or team [option B]?", with two options this product's category really splits on. One short line, under 12 words. Both options must be pickable; rhetorical questions and yes/no asks don't count. NOT a trade-off, NOT a sponsor pitch — a real question with two clear sides.
