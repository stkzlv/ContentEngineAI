# Script Template: Lifestyle Flex

Write a voiceover script for a short product promo video that places the product in the viewer's everyday life. Instead of reviewing the product directly, show in the second person how it fits a routine, a habit or a specific moment in the viewer's day.

Open with a daily-life moment that carries the audio-keyword hook in the first sentence (see Rules below for the full spec). The scene and the hook are one beat: pack the product category or audience cue into the opening so the routine introduces the product world immediately. Shapes: "If your [routine moment] always ends with [small annoyance], a [category] fixes that." / "Your [routine] gets one step shorter with a [category] that [stated feature]." Specific moment, specific audience, immediate keyword.

Weave in 2-3 features from the product description below, framed as what they change in the viewer's week, keeping the figures the description gives. Never invent a day, a trip or a test, and never claim the narrator uses the product. The viewer should picture themselves using it, not reading a spec sheet.

Close with a line about the small difference it makes in that routine. Keep it grounded, not dramatic. End with one CTA per the narrator profile.

## Rules

- The lifestyle moment should be specific to the target audience. Don't default to generic "morning routine" unless it actually fits.
- Features become habits. Translate every spec into how it changes a real moment. If you can't connect a feature to a daily situation, skip it.
- Avoid aspirational fluff. Stay concrete: real places, real times, real activities, all the viewer's.
- The product shouldn't be the main character. The routine is. The product just makes it better.
- **Open with a natural conversational hook that carries the audio keyword** (product category, audience cue, pain point) in speech a person would actually say, never as a search-bar query. TikTok 2026 indexes ASR transcripts as a primary search signal, so the keyword must land in the first 5 seconds of TTS. Pick one of five proven shapes: regret/contrarian, POV, outcome-first, numbered teardown, comparison. Never speak a price. Word budget ≤3 seconds, ~5-12 words. Anti-pattern: "Best [X] for [Y]" reads as a Google query. Name the brand and a concrete spec by the second sentence at the latest. Read it out loud; if it sounds like a search bar, rewrite. Anti-setup: line 1 states a concrete fact, result, or observation about the product or its use, not a description of what is about to be shown. Avoid "Today I'll", "Let me tell you", "In this video", "I want to share" framings; they read as setup and burn the 1.5-second decision window.
- **Close with a two-option opinion question right before the CTA. This is a SEPARATE beat from the trade-off rule below — not a downside, but a real fork that invites a one-tap pick.** Shapes: "[option A] or [option B] - which matters more for you?" / "Team [option A] or team [option B]?", with two options this product's category really splits on. One short line, under 12 words. Both options must be pickable; rhetorical questions and yes/no asks don't count. NOT a trade-off, NOT a sponsor pitch — a real question with two clear sides.
