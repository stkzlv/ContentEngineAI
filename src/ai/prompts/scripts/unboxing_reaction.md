# Script Template: Unboxing Reaction

Write a voiceover script for a short product promo video that walks through what comes in the box and what the listing says about each part, in a calm first-look tone. The narrator read the listing; they never received, unboxed or held the product.

Open with what the box holds and embed the audio-keyword hook in the first sentence (see Rules below for the full spec). Name the product category or audience cue as you start; the low-key tone stays, the keyword lands immediately. Shapes: "Here's what's actually in the box with this [category], per the listing." / "This [category] ships with [stated items], which is more than most." Quiet tone, immediate keyword.

Go through the parts and features the description names, one at a time, with a short, understated note on each: what it's for and whether it seems useful. Let the notes build from the box contents to the details. No need to escalate to excitement.

Close with an honest first take on whether it seems worth a look. Then one CTA per the narrator profile.

## Rules

- Notes should feel low-key. Quiet observations, like thinking out loud, not performing.
- Don't oversell. If a feature is just "fine," say so. Understated appreciation lands better than forced excitement.
- Never describe handling the product (opening, holding, trying it). Focus on what the listing says and what that means.
- Mid-thought pivots are welcome. Keep the tone relaxed and unhurried throughout.
- **Open with a natural conversational hook that carries the audio keyword** (product category, audience cue, pain point) in speech a person would actually say, never as a search-bar query. TikTok 2026 indexes ASR transcripts as a primary search signal, so the keyword must land in the first 5 seconds of TTS. Pick one of five proven shapes: regret/contrarian, POV, outcome-first, numbered teardown, comparison. Never speak a price. Word budget ≤3 seconds, ~5-12 words. Anti-pattern: "Best [X] for [Y]" reads as a Google query. Name the brand and a concrete spec by the second sentence at the latest. Read it out loud; if it sounds like a search bar, rewrite. Anti-setup: line 1 states a concrete fact, result, or observation about the product or its use, not a description of what is about to be shown. Avoid "Today I'll", "Let me tell you", "In this video", "I want to share" framings; they read as setup and burn the 1.5-second decision window.
- **Close with a two-option opinion question right before the CTA. This is a SEPARATE beat from the trade-off rule below — not a downside, but a real fork that invites a one-tap pick.** Shapes: "[option A] or [option B] - which matters more for you?" / "Team [option A] or team [option B]?", with two options this product's category really splits on. One short line, under 12 words. Both options must be pickable; rhetorical questions and yes/no asks don't count. NOT a trade-off, NOT a sponsor pitch — a real question with two clear sides.
