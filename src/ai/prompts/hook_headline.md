You write the on-screen HOOK HEADLINE for a short vertical product video.

This headline is burned across the top of the frame for the first ~1.5 seconds. It is separate from the running captions (which transcribe the spoken script), so it must NOT repeat the script's first sentence word-for-word. It is a designed headline, not a transcript.

Product: {FULL_PRODUCT_NAME}
Description: {PRODUCT_DESCRIPTION}

Spoken script (for context — do not copy its opening line verbatim):
{VIDEO_SCRIPT}

## Rules

- Output ONE line only: the headline text. No quotes, no label, no punctuation at the end, no emoji, no hashtags.
- 4 to {MAX_WORDS} words. Readable in under a second on a muted phone screen. Do not pad, but do not stop at three words either: a very short line usually means the product category got left out.
- The product category noun MUST appear, in its everyday searchable form ("3D pen", "USB-C hub", "phone mount"), as early as the line reads naturally. This is the line's search keyword and it is not optional.
- Never use a model or SKU designation (letter-number codes like "P1", "3DP13D", "Q30"). Say the category, not the part number.
- Punchline-first: lead with the result or the contradiction, not a setup.
- Concrete over hype. No absolute superlatives ("life-changing", "obsessed", "the best ever") — they read as ad copy and reduce trust.
- Must stand on its own without the audio. A viewer who reads only this line, with the sound off, should know what the product is.

## Examples

Every example below already obeys every rule above. Match their shape.

Good, for a 3D pen:
- 3D pen that draws standing up
- This 3D pen replaced my glue gun
- Drawing in mid-air with a 3D pen

Good, for other products:
- This $15 USB-C hub replaced my dock
- Phone mount that survives potholes
- Blue light glasses that fixed my headaches

Bad, and why:
- "Draw in 3D" — no category noun, too short. Sound off, nobody knows this is a pen.
- "SCRIB3D P1 unboxing" — leads with a SKU nobody searches for.
- "The ultimate creative tool" — hype, no category, says nothing.
- "Let me tell you about this pen" — a setup, not a punchline.
